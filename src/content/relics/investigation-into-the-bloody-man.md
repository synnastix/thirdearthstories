*Notes compiled by the Cleric Garland, in cooperation with civil authorities investigating reports of strange activity in the Slums of Timbur Deep.*

The Captain dismisses everything, and I suspect this is due to his contempt for the people of the Slums. Our order has warned them repeatedly that doing so will result in resentment, yet they insist that the people of the Slums manage themselves just fine. I am not quite certain it is simply the people who must be managed.

## The Children’s Reports

In each district the tale is the same, something that has spread and circulated, festering like an open wound. An appropriate description, I must insist, as an open wound is exactly what was reported.

The children heard the story from their parents. A warning in the night:

*Be silent or the Bloody Man will come.*


Their description is consistent. A bald man, lean but not gaunt, small golden hoops piercing his ears. A stained loincloth his only attire, the rest of his body bare. According to the children, the Bloody Man is a man covered in cuts. The tales vary between simple wounds and larger gashes, but the common image is a man in the filthy loincloth who has wounds all over his body. 

Wounds that bleed.  

And the blood smears on everything he touches.  

When the children are sent to bed, their parents warn them to be quiet. Any sound, no matter how small, might lure the Bloody Man to their home. Once summoned, he waits in the dark spaces. Sometimes between doors where no light lives. Sometimes beneath the bed a child lie upon. Very often, impossible as it sounds, the Bloody Man slips into the very walls themselves, inhabiting the space between the children and their parents.

To those who have not experienced the encounter it sounds like a farce. Yet to the children, the *many* children that I have spoken to, the fear is real.

They have heard him.

In the dark of night. When they lie awake. A sound they make draws him near. A whisper to a sibling, a stray laugh. Then…

*BANG! BANG! BANG!*

The thumping in the walls, deep and hollow. The Bloody Man trying to reach for them.

In the morning the children report mothers cleaning when they ordinarily do not. They wipe the walls with wet rags. Smearing crimson stains. The same places where the children heard the noise. They do so mindlessly, as if in a trance, refusing to answer questions the morning after the Bloody Man has passed.

This part of the reports we can confirm. I have spoken with parents who deny any mention of the Bloody Man, even denying having heard of him. The rare few who have confirmed the rumor speak of it as a nightmare, something a child dreamed. I found it curious that so many children may have the same nightmare, especially so when they may be separated by some distance in the Slums so as to never encounter one another.

We have seen the oddity too, the Captain and I. We rushed to one home at the behest of a child who claimed the Bloody Man had visited the night before. When we arrived, we found the child’s mother wiping at the wall mindlessly. The rag she used had already gone dry, dark filth smeared into the boards. When we questioned her, she barely lifted her voice to speak to us, her eyes vacant, her soul elsewhere.

What the Captain has confirmed separately is that children go missing from time to time without a trace. The Captain reports that when the parents are interviewed, they either do not remember the missing child, or do not seem to be bothered by the disappearance. The Captain confessed that this has led to the authorities putting fewer resources into the investigations. 

*“If the parents don’t care, why should we?”*

I have advised Cleric Nisten of these reports. Nisten told me I should follow the Captain’s lead. I disagree, and will continue to report my findings.
