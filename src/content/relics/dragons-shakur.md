*The Twin Dragons of Shakur-Pak is written here as told by the bard Seldanis, who claimed to have spent years traveling with sailors in the southern Misran Sea.*

## The Twin Dragons of Shakur-Pak

In the age before men,  
Before the gods had learned of desire,  
Their powers flowed without care,  
And they set in motion events which would sunder the land.  

The world was smaller then,  
The Misran was more lake than sea,  
Separated from the ocean beyond,  
By the land which encircled it.  

Along its southern edges were twin mountains,  
The land between them a valley,  
They came to it without announcing themselves,  
The twin dragons of Shakur-Pak.  

Lesane, the Dragon of the West,  
Latore, the Dragon of the East,  
They came together and for a long time,  
For a very long time,  
They were friends.  

The dragons spoke to one another,  
Through song and time,  
Circling one another as if the rest of the world did not matter,  
As if it did not care.  

But it did care.

The time of Man had come,  
Men who heard the dragons’ songs,  
The tribe of Shakur, who followed Lesane,  
The tribe of Pak, who followed Latore,  
Each swayed by the dragons’ songs.  

Lesane was declared to be a Prince,  
His followers worshipped him as one would a god or king,  
Lean of body with a voice that revealed true pain,  
Behind eyes that had gone cold.  

Latore’s followers, too, worshipped their dragon-king,  
He was larger than Lesane and dark as night,  
He became notorious for a voice that could calm waves,  
Who sang with a smoothness unmatched.

Each tribe claimed their patron superior to the other.  
Each drew their master toward conflict.  

The Twins called for peace,  
The tribes did not listen,  
They could only hear the dragons’ songs,  
Not the words behind them,  
Soon pride turned sour,  
And the tribes went to war.

What started with words and song,  
Soon flowed to followers with fists and stone,  
In the valley between mountains,  
The chief of Lesane was killed.  

Revenge came swiftly,  
As the skirmishes continued,  
Blood stained the valley floor,  
And the chief of Latore lay dead too.

The Twins wept,  
They met in secret,  
Both wanted to end the pain,  
The strife,  
They came up with a plan.

On the hottest day of the year,  
A shadow blocked out the sun,  
As darkness cloaked the land,  
Lesane clutched the western mountain,  
Latore the east,  
And they pulled with all their might.  

The valley between cracked open,  
Ripped apart,  
The land between the Misran and the open sea parted,  
Water rushed in to fill the void.

In doing so,  
The Twins spent their last essence,  
They lay at the top of the mountains,  
And took their final rest.

From that moment on,  
The tribes have been held from one another,  
Separated by the vast distance between,  
Where space and time let tempers cool.

Today we still worship the Twins,  
Sing their songs of legend,  
The Prince and the Notorious one,  
Live forever in the hearts of men.
