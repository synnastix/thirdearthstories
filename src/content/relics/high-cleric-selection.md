*The Temple selects their senior members carefully, and rarely discloses the criteria used in that process. This report was discovered in the quarters of the Senior Cleric Cimron, assistant to Hierophant Hermanus, during the investigation into Cimron’s death in the Year 695 CR. The records show he was reported missing after failing to appear for his assigned duties for three consecutive days. He was never found.*

## Dossier Contents

### History of Service

Hannaren, born in Merrow’s Reach  
Senior Cleric  
25 years of service to the Temple Order  

Initiate, years 667 to 668 CR. Hannaren entered into service in Merrow’s Reach, where his dedication to service in the name of Sovalis was exemplary. Hannaren readily adapted to Temple teachings and structure, and engaged in various activities to promote the Temple and Empire in the autonomous city.

Acolyte, years 668 to 670 CR. Despite having no formal upbringing within the Temple, Hannaren quickly memorized Sovalis’ teachings to such a degree that established Clerics would consult him to locate specific verses in the Book of Beginnings to prepare for rituals and services. As an Acolyte, he pursued the healing arts faithfully, becoming experienced in both herbal and spiritual healing practices.

Cleric, years 670 to 676 CR. Hannaren’s aptitude for healing attracted the attention of senior Temple members, and he was transferred to Timbur Deep to study under the masters. In that capacity he excelled, cultivating a reputation one of the best practitioners. His work with the alchemist Xanion on ferns found in the northern valley led to the development of a new compound useful in treating severe burns, which was of great value to the Legion.

Senior Cleric, years 676 to 692 CR. Hannaren directly contributed to the expansion of Temple influence within the Heartlands upon reassignment to the Temple in Merrow’s Reach. Hannaren worked with local leaders to support the Listener’s Symposium, in which the Empire has interest as a potential school for educating Legion officers. 

Hannaren’s dedication to knowledge, as well as his skills in the healing arts, reflects great credit upon the teachings of Sovalis, the Temple, and the Empire.

### Inquisitor’s Evaluation

The Inquisitor’s Office has learned of no concerns regarding Hannaren’s service, character, or personal relations which would negatively impact the Temple or the Empire.

### Dissenting Remarks

*“I commend Hannaren in his service to the Temple and the Empire. However, it is my recommendation that Hannaren remain in service as a Senior Cleric until such time that a Warden position becomes available. Hannaren’s service to expand Temple influence in Merrow’s Reach reflects greatly on his ability to represent the Temple and the Empire in a city that has historically sought to maintain its political independence. As such, it is my belief that Hannaren’s service as a Warden, a position of status equivalent to the role of High Cleric, would be a more appropriate station.”* – Hierophant Callum, Timbur Deep

### Final Determination

The Senary of Hierophants has determined that Hannaren’s petition for elevation to the position of High Cleric of the Temple Order is approved by a vote of 4-2 in favor of the decision. Additionally, Grand Hierophant Sanus provided the following statement:

*“It pleases me greatly to see such a marvelous example of servitude to both the Temple and the healing arts. While I agree with the suggestion that Hannaren would make an excellent Warden, it is my judgement that his contributions warrant bestowing the coveted title of High Cleric upon him. I look forward to his continued service and, may he long live, and should he have the aspiration, he may rise to the rank of Hierophant before the end of his days.”*

In accordance with tradition, Hierophants are hereby directed to notify their subordinate Wardens of Hannaren’s newly appointed status as High Cleric of the Temple Order, such that they may not interfere with his duties should he arrive in their respective Domains and instead welcome him as an equal.
