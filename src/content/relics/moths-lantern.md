*When a mortal gives up their soul to the world, it follows the light of the moon toward the afterlife. Between the here and there, the Hounds are charged with judgement – guarding the way or shepherding the lost as they see fit. What follows is an excerpt from the tome “The Book of Beginnings,” the official doctrine of the Temple.*

## The Creation of the Heavens

Sovalis, god of wisdom, and Synnaren, goddess of love and desire, witnessed the birth of Sensa-nai, goddess of death, and the world began to fill with the spirits of the lost. They watched as Sensa-nai held the dead and dying until their bodies returned to the clay of the earth, ready to be remolded into something anew. 

Sensa-nai wept for the souls that were left to wander. She found as many as she could, one by one, helping them to find a new body, so that they may become alive again. Yet there were too many, far too many, and she could not find them all. Annuriel, goddess of war and maker of bodies, shaped new forms but Somar-dani, god of the sky, in his incessant wandering, continued to breathe new life into the shapes of men. As such, the world soon became filled with souls who had no refuge.

Synnaren held Sensa-nai as her tears fell. She appealed to Sovalis, who had denied her comfort many times already. Sovalis’s heart remained still, but even he could see that the world would soon be crowded with too many souls. Thus he proposed to her that the gods create a new domain to hold the spirits of men, one separate from the world.

Together they approached the Senary. Synnaren once again whispered to them.

To Anpur-dim, god of light: “Dear brother, your work is tireless. All day you walk across the sky, and at night you battle the darkness. I see how you tire. How your light fades. Will you not give the Pale Lantern to Sensa-nai so that you may rest?”

The god of light had no interest in the lost souls, but he had grown tired and desired rest. He granted Synnaren’s request, and gifted the moon to Sensa-nai, who filled with joy. She claimed the moon whose paleness matched that of the wandering souls.  Sensa-nai declared to make it a paradise which she called *the heavens*, for to her the moon would be a gateway and behind it was a multitude. In honor of Synnaren’s help, Sensa-nai permitted lost lovers to be reunited, families to find children, and the exiled to find communion. Indeed, the heavens would become a place where one could find their heart’s desire.

While Sensa-nai built the heavens beyond the Pale Lantern, Synnaren whispered to her brother to secure passage for the lost.

To Somar-dani, god of the sky: “Sweet brother, see how your granddaughter cries for them? For the creations into which you breathed life? Will you not grant their souls passage so that they may ascend?”

Somar-dani agreed. He believed the souls of men to be like smoke, something that clung to him as he passed and he was glad to be rid of them.

Sensa-nai rejoiced.

## Abandonment

The souls of men began to flow into the sky toward the Pale Lantern, and while they had the blessing of Somar-dani, they caught the attention of another. Kalinexis, god of storms, born of rage. Kalinexis swept over man and separated the soul from the shell with delight. Now, the souls lifted into the sky which they had not done before. He saw it as another offense, a reflection of impermanence and abandonment. He became angry, and went to his mother, Aloni-lei, goddess of the sea.

To Aloni-lei: “Mother, how can this be? You have never held me, and you allow my father to abandon me. Now you allow man to leave me as well?”

Aloni-lei was no friend to man, and she had created many creatures in the sea so that man should stay away from her. She heard the cries of her son, and even if she could not hold him, she felt his pain. She knew Anpur-dim well and had seen the way the moon shifted over the days as his power faded. She whispered into the world, linking the souls of men to the light itself. Thus, as the Pale Lantern’s light dimmed, it was easier for souls to lose their way and become stranded. 

This satisfied Kalinexis and he, too, would interfere where he could, blocking the light with storm clouds and trapping the souls of men.

## The Void Hounds

Sensa-nai wept once more for the trapped souls, and consulted with Sovalis and Synnaren. Synnaren once again appealed to her brother.

To Sovalis: “Dear brother, I ask not for myself but for Sensa-nai. For man. So that they may have what they desire. Oh dear brother who understands the world and its suffering, will you not lend your aid?”

Sovalis did understand the world and its suffering. He had already imparted his essence unto man so that man, too, could understand. Within him had grown a desire for man to come to understand wisdom. He recognized an opportunity, a second chance for man to understand.

They could not dispel Aloni-lei’s curse, instead formed an alliance. They shaped the void between worlds to form the Hounds – beasts who would be charged as both guard and escort. Sovalis shaped Hunter, the leader of the pack and tracker of lost souls. Synnaren shaped Bandit, the sneak who could evade the gaze of other gods. While Sensa-nai shaped Whisper, the pup.

The Void Hounds would appear similar to the best herding dogs men knew, bearing a wolf’s sharp gaze and a fox’s cunning. Their faces cloaked in black, split by a narrow blaze of white running from brow to muzzle, and a snowy ruff framing their necks. In this way the lost souls would recognize them easily, and follow them to ascension.

They waited patiently in the space between worlds as the moon faded from full light to complete darkness. As souls made the journey toward the Pale Lantern, the Hounds would pass judgement on them according to the agreement between the three. Any soul found unworthy would be carried away by Sensa-nai who delivered them to her mother, Annuriel. The goddess of war would erase their memory and impart their soul into another shell, so that they may be given another chance to experience the world.

As the light of the Pale Lantern returned, day by day, the Hounds waited and watched. When the moon was at its fullest, the Hounds raced to the world. There, they rounded up souls who could not find their way, nipping at them and herding them toward the heavens. Hunter and Bandit led souls with ease, while Whisper, who was still learning, occasionally led them astray. Then, when the night ended, they waited patiently for the next full moon.

In this way balance was struck for man. Those who were judged worthy entered the heavens, while those who were not were given another chance to learn life’s lessons.
