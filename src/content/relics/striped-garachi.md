*A journal entry from the merchant Darrel, of House Radis, recorded during an expedition into the northern valley in the year 703 CR.*

Madam Sassui will need to watch her pockets, lest Daphne drain them to get her hands on this adorable creature!

The Visari call it the *striped garachi*, and when I asked if that meant there were other variants their grins said everything. As it turns out, there are, and the garachi mainly vary in pattern and color. What remains distinctive are the rounded ears, oversized for the animal, and the dark patches over large amber-colored eyes. I am told they commonly bear a white stripe running down their short muzzle, and I cannot help but be reminded of the Void Hounds when I look at them.

It is a small, stocky creature whose fur is thick, causing it to appear larger than it really is. The striped variant has grey-brown fur with faint stripes along its back. Their toes are elongated almost like fingers, and a thumb on each paw allows it to grip branches easily. Its rump is punctuated by a long, slender tail that flicks about like that of a common cat. Also like the cat, the garachi’s tail seems to communicate its mood, which ranges from curious to indifferent toward wanderers in the forest.

Wild garachi are attracted to the Visari settlements clinging to the trunks of the large, red-wooded trees in the northern valley. There, they descend from the high branches to pilfer scraps of fruit, nuts, or vegetables, often rummaging through Visari compost piles like vermin. Curiously, they also eat the grubs and insects that help break down the rotting vegetation. I have been fortunate enough to witness this personally during my stay with the Visari and for their part, the Visari tolerate the intrusion. They believe the garachi bring fortune onto the villages, and will sometimes leave small offerings of fresh food for them near the compost piles.

Several Visari that I have observed kept garachi as pets. They have a somewhat lazy temperament, often lying on high branches or the rooftops of the Visari village. This changes quickly if other vermin approach the area – I have seen them quickly spring into action whenever a squirrel or rat trespassed into their territory, using their front paws to fling the intruder out of the trees.

I must get closer to the Visari who handle these tiny beasts and learn how they are bred. I am quite certain that if we can bring breeding stock to Timbur Deep, we can make a small fortune selling them to anyone whose daughter loves cute things.
