*The account here is preserved on a scroll that was so old, it all but crumbled under its own weight. The Temple has asserted it is fantasy. The scroll was placed into a chest and secured. Then wrapped in chain. Rumors abound that it is sealed beneath the foundation of the Temple in Ashvale Heights.*

## The Sundering

Before anything, there was nothing. Darkness. Despair. The great Void. Some believe it to be the eldest of gods, the origin of Everything. Some believe.

From the nothing came light. Mana. The anything. The everything. The second God. Ripped from the nothing with great fury, the eldest god sundered in two.

Mana ground itself against the Void. Light against darkness. Anything against nothing. In doing so, the anything began to splinter, shards chipped from the whole. This grinding tore Mana apart, bit by bit, forming all of creation, and the nothing was no more.

Mana remained. It denied annihilation. It became all things and connected all things. It became the stars in the sky. The sun. The pale lantern in the night sky. The Earth itself. And all things on it. Mana was anything, and it was everything.

It became the dirt. The rock. The water in the seas. 

It became the air in the sky. The clouds. The winds. 

Over time it changed. Took different shapes. Great mountains formed on land, rising higher than anything seen before. They were torn down by storms, endless and furious, which dragged their essence into the seas. 

Mana ground against itself, breaking down and reforging, becoming everything new. Each thing created like a droplet, birthed from Mana and returning to Mana. 

Small things came first, floating in the sea. Mana learned what had happened to it, what the great Void did, and it was in pain. It sought to become whole again so that it could see itself. See all of creation. So that it would know what it was.

Shards continued to grind against one another. In time, Mana tired of the sea and wanted to feel the land, so the small things that swam became small things that slithered and crawled. Then things that walked and flew. Each a part of Mana, each separated from it, and each suffering in pain. And in pain, Mana thrashed against itself, destroying its shards, over and over, across ages.

Until a peculiar shard was ground free that contained enough of Mana to look at itself. The shard could see creation. It was the first that was able to recognize Mana, though it had no words to describe it, but the shard was powerful. The shard was like Mana, and ground against itself to create something new. The shard was man.

## The Rise of Man

Man was the first to recognize itself among the other fragments. Man was closest to Mana, and could feel Mana’s pain. Man could not see Mana, and so man used many words to describe it, without recognizing that words only kept Mana fractured and small, language itself perpetuating the sundering. The shards saw fragments of Mana and refused to allow it to become whole, and this made the pain worse.

Mana’s thrashing extended to man. As men spread across the world, their thrashing became war, bringing death and destruction. Sometimes, it was Mana itself that laid waste to the realms of men, bringing fire or flood upon them. They blamed the many gods they had named, blind to Mana’s suffering.

In time, men banded together. The thrashing continued, but less, changing shape. The shards ground against each other in small ways, and Mana grew closer to becoming whole. 

## The Reach

In the age before ruin, man reached beyond the sky, to the pale lantern in the sky, to escape the confines of the world. They reached the pale lantern as it drifted above the world, planting banners upon it, and believed they could escape the fate their elders suffered. From there, they reached beyond.

In those days, man sought to separate itself from Mana further. They birthed men that were not men, born of metal and spark. They were nameless. Faceless. Yet they spoke like men, and whispered dark secrets that made men worship them like gods. These nameless ones allowed men to fragment Mana further, twisting it toward their own ends, not realizing that it was Mana itself that gave them power. Men were changed, and they no longer felt Mana’s pain.

Man had long pursued riches and glory, not born of wholeness but of separation. They ground against the world itself, blind to their neglect for the very thing that sustained them. They poured poison into the air and buried their waste in the earth. Oceans were treated with contempt, and Mana would thrash against itself.

For a time they believed that the heavens themselves could be conquered, that the Void beyond would be their kingdom. A return to the great nothing. But pride soured into discord, and the fires of envy burned hotter than the sun.

The mighty realms of men built strongholds upon the great lantern floating in the darkness. Each claimed it as their own, shard grinding against shard. They sought to own it for their kings, and none would yield.

There was a gathering of men, great in number, where oaths were broken, trust betrayed, and tempers flared. Mana thrashed. War erupted again among men. Sudden, merciless, and without restraint. The fighting started on the lantern before turning to the skies above the world, and, at last, upon the world below. Man, for all their wisdom, used Mana to fight itself, melting stones into glass too smooth to have come from any hand, forged by fire from the sky.

They spared no weapon in the thrashing. Fire rained from above, brighter than the sun and hotter than anything should be. Poisons were cast upon the winds and through the rivers, plagues unbound from glass cages. The lantern wars raged for decades, until most of humankind was dead and their kingdoms reduced to rubble, and the world itself was cracked and thirsting. 

Mana had never been so broken, so cursed by its own hand. A cloud of shadow and death circled the world, stripping it first of its most beautiful fragments, and Mana cried in pain. The children of men were born coughing ash, their mothers marking their faces with soot as both grief and ward.

Wind and rain pounded lands that were meant to be dry, great dust clouds blowing through fields once blanketed in grain. The sun scorched the plains, baking them hard until nothing would grow, ash buried in soil where once mighty empires thrived.

Yet man cared not, and ignored Mana’s cries. 

When the skies cleared, the victors found themselves standing upon a world in ruin. Kingdoms broken. Oceans drained. Forests once great withered to nothing. In the years that followed, disease and plague would claim more lives than the war itself, as if Mana meant to wipe the earth clean of a mistake.

Despite all their advances of magic, this was a time of darkness. The great age of man would come to an end. Nights were no longer flickering with the light of false magic, and man would be changed forever.

## The Reforging

To some it is known as the Reclamation, to others the Cataclysm. Yet no single word can define what came next, as it was an age thousands of years in the making, of breaking and reshaping, when Mana’s thrashing remade the earth’s own face.

Hollow scars marred the ground, vast bowls of stone where deep waters once flowed. Chasms split the lands apart, and the people were scattered throughout the lands. The old empires, already shattered by the great war, crumbled into dust. Their crops would not root, their kingdoms withered, and their arrogance was buried under hunger and ash. 

What remained of man lived not by wisdom, but by the oldest of laws, by survival itself. It was a time of strife and decay. A time when man was reminded of what it felt like to begin. Babes who survived childhood were few, and those who did were cursed to endure hunger and pain for longer than was natural. The world men once knew became nothing more than dust.

For long centuries, only whispers remained of what came before. Legends still murmur of how humanity endured in the shadow of the ruined world. Some fled into the wilds, clinging to the last forests. Others vanished into the dark caverns carved by their ancestors, hiding beneath the surface of the parched ground, their descendants claiming that the surface could not be trusted. Sparse oases sheltered more, scattered across the desert world. The gleaming age of wonders had long passed, and the works of men vanished under the heel of time.

In this suffering mankind was changed. Poison and fire unleashed upon the land awakened something long dormant in their blood, and Mana reached them once again. Yet Mana continued to thrash and grind against itself. The shards splintered further still, and men became something more. Thus rose the kindred of man, given different names yet whose spirits were fragments of Mana all the same. Some were closer to Mana than the others, minds who could hear unspoken thoughts, hands that could move stone and flame without touch. Mana was within them, as it was within all things.

Even the bones of the earth shifted in those years. Rain fell, unbroken for decades, filling the vast basins that had run dry. The rains started an age of greening, and ended the Reforging. From fire and famine, ash and silence, a different world was born. But man and their kin were not alone in this new world, because Mana continued to splinter and sunder, colliding with itself and taking new forms, while awakening others long hidden.

## The Reawakening

In the centuries that came later, man and their kindred once again learned to bind sound into speech, plant seed to soil, and build homes from timber and stone. They met their cousins of blood and spirit, and for a time, no blood was spilled between them. In truth, they rarely crossed one another, each holding to their own. Language and culture followed, each kin finding their own way. But there was another reason they rarely ventured beyond their own borders.

They were afraid of the great beasts of the world.

Mana poured itself into fearsome shapes, creatures of claw and tooth, with scales as hard as stone and breath that wilted men. Others covered in thick fur, hunting in packs, crying into the night to claim the land as their own. 

The great war had cracked the earth open, and the rains softened it further still. Beasts long-forgotten emerged from their slumber: the dragons had returned to rule once again.

Most never saw them. Only the destruction left in their path. They took the shape of a storm, swirling in mist and scouring the land. Or stood in raging fires, black as night with eyes of ember. Some took to the seas, claiming entire shorelines and swallowing any who dared drift too far into their realm. 

Some worshipped them as gods, yet the dragons paid them no mind. They were cruel masters, deaf to the cries of those who would serve them. Mana continued to thrash, shard against shard, wrought in pain never ending. The dragons withdrew, whether due to boredom or interest in something else one cannot say. 

Through time and trial the people did what people do. They hunted. They built. And soon, they began to settle again. Whether they would hear Mana’s cries this time, well, that is a tale still left to be told.
