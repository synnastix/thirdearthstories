*The origin of this document is unclear, but it is among the earliest of records entered in the Imperial Archives. It was decided, then, that some things are worth preserving, so their lessons may not be forgotten.*

## Tears of the Gods

No one knew what we had done. 

How we had displeased them.

What ritual had failed.

What god felt offended.

But that year, the gods wept harder than any other. Not just that year – the year that followed as well. Two long years of rain and snow. Rivers spilled their banks, the Sova and the Vrana both, stretching tendrils across the land. Fields rotted. Cattle died.

In the city, the humble city of Timbur Deep, the fools remained. Cloistered in their homes. Sheltered from the rain. Rich and poor alike, complaining about the muck and the mud. Oh how they failed to see the disappointment of the gods. 

It was only the beginning.

## The Hunger

The poor suffered first.

They always do.

Two loaves of bread became one. 

One became none.

Those without farms fought over scraps. Those with armies fought over farms. The poor dregs in the city, corpses who walked, who fell, who cried in pain. Those who had no more strength to cry simply died in the mud. In the time that followed, death came to the few, but it would not be restrained.

## A Spreading Rot

The waters ran black. 

Filled with rot and decay. 

It spread to the people.

There was no escape.

Soon, death came for everyone. Rich or poor. It did not matter.

It started as a cough. One dry and wheezing. Next the fire. The fire that burned within. The fire that took men’s sanity and took control of their dreams. The wheeze turned wet, the cough came with blood. Then, the lesions… the terrible, itching lesions.

In time, the rot would take half the city, caring not for fit or status. The rot took them all.

## The Cleansing

We cry for them still.

The ones who were culled.

A noble sacrifice.

To save the city.

The rot did not stop. No cleric could cure it. It had to be stopped. The choice was made.

The city was cleansed of infection in the only sure way. The only way that saved the lives of many. The people did what had to be done. A friend. A brother. A son. A lover.

It did not matter.

And it worked.

## Restoration

The gods were pleased.

The city did what it must.

With great pain and suffering.

The city survived.

A sign from the gods as their weeping ceased. They showed mercy on the people. They dried the lands. The rivers returned to their banks. And that year, that next beautiful year, every birth was blessed, and no baby was lost among the chosen.

Every year since, when the storms come, the people renew their allegiance to the gods, never forgetting what happened then, in that dark time.
