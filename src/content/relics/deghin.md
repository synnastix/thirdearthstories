*Once a secret of the Nirashi, gifted to the Emperor Timur the Bold, in the Year 132 CR as a tribute. This recipe has passed through generations in one form or another, finding its way into taverns and camps around the Heartlands.*

This bitter brew has become a favorite among Legion soldiers and others whose duties involve difficult tasks.

## Gathering and Preparation of Ingredients

### Acorns
Acorns are the main component of the brew, the seed of the oak, and drop from trees when the heat of summer passes and the weather starts to turn. These can simply be gathered by hand and stored for use when ready.

Raw acorns make a bad brew, and worse stomach. The Nirashi simply place the acorns into a sack and leave them in a stream for no less than two days. Alchemists claim this is not sufficient, and describe a process of boiling the acorns whole, dumping the water, then boiling again. There is some debate over how many times to repeat the process, with some who claim seven washes make the best flavor.

Once rinsed, the acorns are cracked and skinned, then left to dry in the sun or near a fire.

### Chicory
Chicory root is the second component, and it is of significant importance to select the correct root when harvesting. The plant is easiest to identify through its blooms in late summer, where the blue flowers expand their many petals. If the flowers have ruffles instead of distinct petals, it is to be avoided. The roots must be flexible – one dense as wood cannot be used. A simple washing and peeling suffice, then left to dry like the acorns. Splitting the root is sometimes done to dry faster.

### Barley
The Nirashi call it orzha. Purchase from a farmer, it is cheap and not worth trying to gather in the fields.

## Roasting and Grinding
All three ingredients must be roasted simply. This can be done in a simple pan over fire, but care must be taken so as not to scorch them as that will sour the flavor. Do not combine before roasting as each responds differently to the flame.

The traditional method is to roast first, then grind against a stone with a hard wooden rod. A mortar and pestle is better for those who have it. 

## Brewing
Combine roughly three parts acorn with one part root, then steep in hot water. Add barley powder to suit the guest’s taste.
