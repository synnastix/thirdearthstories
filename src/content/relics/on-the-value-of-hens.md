*Compiled by the young mage Heliana, who reported being mocked by her mentor when she proposed the idea that the hen was as crucial to the Empire as the sword.*

To whomever (I do not care),

I have heard the doubts. The mockery. The wishes of others that I follow the Temple’s teachings to abandon the scrolls, and don the apron.

I shall heed them not.

I understand the hen. Often overlooked and undervalued when compared to the auroch or goat. Even the hare, they say: “oh, it’s better for meat.”

Wrong. Every single one, wrong in so many ways. Indulge me, for a moment, and allow me to enlighten you to the truth of it.

## A Ferocious Guardian
A hen, raised correctly, is a most curious creature. It will follow you as its mother, and you must teach it like a child. To scratch at the ground. To peck at the thing that needs pecked. They watch and they learn. One could watch them for hours as they move through the world, always looking, always learning. They will even speak with you, in fact almost constantly. If you have a hen, it is very difficult to become lonely, even when you are otherwise alone.

And they will guard you well, once you have earned their trust. 

Some say the cat is the woman’s ally. Many go so far as to compare them to women. Clerics will tell you they can ward off curses and death. They claim the killing of mice as proof.

But did you know that the hen will kill mice too? And every crawling thing that might threaten a home.

I am sure you did not, if you live in the city, but the farmers know. They will tell you. They survive off the garden, eating insects that would otherwise destroy crops. They chase away the small things that steal a harvest. Within their domain they will not tolerate intrusion, whether snake or rat, and they will show no mercy. 

A hen is a tiny dragon, after all.

## Warmth without Fire
Have you ever been cold in the winter?

Of course you have.

Have you ever slid your fingers into a hen’s feathers in winter?

Most people think without fur or wool one is doomed, but all one must do is get under the surface of the hen’s feathers to feel the falsehood. The heat is like a fire, warm beyond the creature’s size. 

In fact, their feathers prove most useful whether shed or plucked when prepared for meals. Stuffed beneath blankets. Tucked into heavy winter clothes. Made into quills for writing. It is the hen that has saved many from the harshest of winter cold, yet they receive no recognition for doing so.

## Food without Season
Other animals have seasons.

A cow births a calf once a year, then must wait all winter to birth another. Sheep are the same.

Not the hen.

The hen will lay her eggs all year long. While she may slow, she will never tire, not until after years of faithful service. When she finally takes rest, she becomes the most delicious of soups, her spirit strong enough to bless the weak and cure sickness. 

There is another dish, a favorite in the central valleys of the Heartlands, that can help even in the harshest of winters. It is called grau drobi, sometimes just drobi, and is prepared thusly:

---

### Grau Drobi
Four palms of wheat berries  
Four eggs  
A bit of milk  

Wash the berries of wheat until clean and then boil. Strain the water and keep only the berries. Separately, into a pot prepare milk and egg in equal measure, mixing together until they can no longer be told apart. Warm the pan and stir, until small bubbles form at the edges. Then mix in the wheat berries and cook until it sticks to the spoon. 

---

As a basic meal, drobi can sustain a person for months and even years. Of course, if one tired of the taste, it is easy to make it special by adding cheese, vegetables, and, for the wealthy, even other meats such as chopped bits of hog or cow.

## Political Protections
I should not need to remind you but I shall anyway – hens are respected by many domain Lords and as such, the penalties for murdering a hen that is not yours carries heavy fines. For some, the fine is not just for the hen that was lost, but for two generations of hens that would have followed. How many that equates to varies by domain, but I believe it is proof of the importance of hens as no other beast receives such protection. Further, exactly no domain protects people the same, which further demonstrates the value of the hen.

## A Heroine
The evidence cannot be dismissed. The hen saves more lives every single year than swords. They add economic value to every household, providing food and protecting crops that might otherwise be ravaged. The hen, the precious hen, the dismissed heroine of the realm.

Are you not convinced?
