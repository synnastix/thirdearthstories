*Statement provided by the sailor Miffen, found adrift in a ruined merchant vessel near the eastern coast of the Misran Sea. The survivor was rescued by a ship chartered by House Merris to explore the Southern Isles, however it returned to Merrow’s Reach immediately after the encounter. The Captain returned the charter deposit and refused to say anything further. The statement was annotated by scholars at the Academy before being submitted to the Imperial Archives.*

## The Fire

It was Wickam that started it, the old fool. He refused to take the usual precautions, thought it would delight the men to smell roasted meat on deck as they rowed. Or as a reward for their rowing. It doesn’t matter.

We’d been at sea for days. Made sure to stay in the light blue water, never the dark where the drowned fangs live. Never too close to shore either. Everyone knows what that means.

*Miffen remained quiet for a long moment, keeping his gaze on the floor. The interviewer waited for him to resume his story.*

The sail was full and everyone was drinking. Laughing. A celebration of sorts. What we were celebrating I do not know. That we were alive perhaps. We barely noticed when it started.

An ember, or spark. Something from the brazier. It rose past the meat, cast into the air either by the ship’s rocking, or maybe something dropped into the coals and caused one to pop. It landed on the sail and found a fold near the mast. The smell came before the smoke, but the flame didn’t wait long. Soon the entire sail had become engulfed, still full of wind but now a great wall of flames.

*Miffen laughed softly, seemingly amused.*

Perhaps it was fed by the wind. I don’t know. There was nothing we could do. It was too hot, burned too fast, and it was gone in a flash. Ash settled on the deck and we found ourselves adrift.

## The Storm

Everyone just looked at each other like we couldn’t believe what had just happened. We looked at Wickam, who stared at the brazier like it could be judged for a crime. Our ship had slowed but the wind did not. In fact, it picked up slightly, dark clouds rolling in. Then a thunderclap in the distance.

Gavin shouted from near the railing. The ship was drifting toward the darker water. We did what any good sailors would do in that situation.

Panicked.

The Captain ordered everyone to oars. We pulled hard while he shifted course, leading us closer to shore. Rain started to fall around us. First it came as droplets. Soon it felt less like rain, and more like the ocean falling from the sky. Some breaths came half-full of water, and sailors choked as they gasped for breath, desperate to get away from the deep.

The ship rocked hard as the waves rose and fell. Sometimes the oars weren’t even in the water and pulled back too easily. We fought for what seemed like ages, but found our way to a space where the river had created a delta and several small islands stuck above the waves.

*Miffen laughed again.*

## The Wreck

I use the word *island* loosely here, most were barely more than rocks. But on one of the larger outcroppings in the distance we saw something odd. A square rising above it, distinct and unlike the jagged shapes around it. As we approached, we made out a ship that looked to be lodged on the rock. It hadn’t been there long, the sail still intact, but the ship itself was ruined.

The Captain thought we should inspect it. If there were survivors, we could help them. If there were not, their sail could help us. The storm had spent itself by then, slowing to a gentle breeze. The clouds were dark enough to block the sun and rain still fell, but the worst of it had passed. Our ship approached the wreckage, careful not to run atop the rock ourselves. We were within twenty yards of the vessel when the Captain called out.

There was no response.

He and some of the others called out again and again. Never came any calls back to us.

“Never get out of the boat.”

That was what every sailor knew about this stretch of coastline. Not until you got closer to Merrow’s Reach. 

Yet there we were, tying a rope to Devin who’d volunteered to swim to the wreckage. He was to go to the ship and cut the sail free. Then bundle it and secure it with the rope. Then we could drag him back to the ship and use the sail to help finish the trip.

*Miffen sniffled.*

I liked Devin. He had a wife. Three children. 

*Miffen sighed.*

He climbed down a rope and dropped into the water. It wasn’t safe to just jump in with the rocks so close. Devin swam quickly while we kept a hand on the rope, ready to pull him back if anything went wrong. We watched the water carefully. It was littered with debris from the wreck, other things just floating by.

It didn’t take him long to reach the ship. It lay crooked against the rock, the deck tilted slightly away from us. We saw him climb up the rope netting on the side of the hull, up to the deck railing, then drop over the other side.

Then we waited.

## The River Drake

For a good bit nothing happened. We could see the bottom of the sail being tugged, cut free at the rings. While Devin worked, we prepared to pull him back through the water. Then, without warning, the rope went tight and pulled free from Wickam’s hand. We rushed to grab it before it completely went loose in the water. It pulled tight, then snapped back, completely slack. We watched the end of it fall back over the edge of the wrecked hull and drop into the water.

We hadn’t heard anything. No scream. No rustling. We thought perhaps Devin had gotten snagged and untied himself to get free. We were not ready for what came next.

There was a tremendous splash of water in the debris next to our ship. Something enormous slammed into the side of our hull, near the railing. Something that hit it and held. The ship was pushed to the side, rocking, then started to tip toward whatever had latched on, like we were being dragged downward.

The drake was so fast. It didn’t wait for the ship to tip over, it climbed up the side, claws splintering the wood as it dug in. When it came over the rail, it snapped Wickam nearly in half, shaking its head violently to finish the job. What didn’t get tossed went down the creature’s throat. 

Several men tried to fight it. They reached for knives or clubs, whatever they could find. Others jumped over the opposite side.

There was no fighting it.

It climbed over the railing and…

*Miffen coughed. Sniffled again.*

I’d never seen that level of carnage. Bodies broken like stalks of dried grass. Like we were nothing. It thrashed on the deck and broke the mast. Destroyed most of the oars. There was nothing left.

*The interviewer asked if Miffen jumped into the water to escape the drake. Miffen laughed as if the question were absolutely ridiculous.*

No, I hid in the cabin. I was lucky to be close enough that when the drake hit, I fell backward into the door. When I saw the drake climb over the edge and start tearing into people, I kicked the door shut and locked it as quickly as I could. Then I hid in the opposite corner.

*Miffen went silent again for a long moment. Tears began to form even as his eyes stayed vacant.*

I had to hear them. Listen to their screams as they were eaten. I heard the splashing of those trying to get away.

They screamed too.

Then, after a while, nothing but the creaking of the ship.

## The Aftermath

I tried to stay silent. I could hear it move on the deck from time to time, feel the ship lean to one side or another. Heard the sickening crunch of it feeding. The ship leaned hard to one side as it moved to the edge, then lurched back as it dropped off into the water. I dared not leave the cabin.

*Miffen’s account is consistent with what we know of the river drake’s hunting behaviors. They are ambush predators, lying just below the surface with their eyes watching for movement. Their nostrils are behind their eyes at the top of the skull, leaving a very faint signature at the top of the water when hunting. It would have been easy for the sailors to mistake the river drake’s hunting silhouette for shipwreck debris.*

They found me a few days later. I told them about the river drake. About how it didn’t just stay in the river and came to hunt us at sea. They asked about the cargo and I told them I didn’t care about it, I just wanted to get back to Merrow’s Reach. The Captain asked if I’d sign a paper for him.

*Miffen laughed.*

Like I’d care at that point. I told him I couldn’t read but that wouldn’t stop me from signing whatever document I needed to sign to get out of there.

*The interviewer asked about the disposition of the cargo. Miffen shrugged.*

Ask the Captain.

*River drakes continue to plague the eastern shore of the Misran Sea, particularly where the river empties into the ocean. Unlike the crocodilians further south near the swamps, river drakes are able to climb and board ships that stray too close to shore. Vessels sailing this region should be advised and stay further out to sea while exercising caution to avoid the deep water as well. Both threats make traversal through this region extremely dangerous.*
