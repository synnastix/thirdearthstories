*The following is the accepted history of the Temple’s gods, excerpted from the tome “The Book of Beginnings”. The Temple accepts followers of other gods, but these are the gods the Temple’s clerics support.*

## The Rise of the Senary

It was in the days before men drew meaning from voice.  
Before dragons took flight.  
Before the wind brushed over the plains, and before the sun kissed the sand.  
It was the beginning. Before time.  
Before anything.  
There was the wyrd.  
The wyrd began to shape existence into all things.    
The earth became the first something and with it, the Senary came into being.  
Three of them gods.  
Three more as goddesses.  
Each given charge by the wyrd as it saw fit.  

**Anpur-dim** - The god of light. The yellow sun who brings warmth to the world. The great watcher who breaks the darkness. He keeps his pace and pays no mind to the cares of others.

**Somar-dani** - The god of the sky. The breath of life. He is the endless wanderer who brings constant change.

**Sovalis** - The god of wisdom. The awakened one. He is the knower of things, and bearer of the world’s suffering.

**Aloni-lei** - The goddess of the sea. The vast expanse. She is the keeper of secrets and creator of great beasts.

**Synnaren** - The goddess of love. The instigator. She is desire personified, the goddess who blesses the world with want, unable to feel its warmth herself.

**Annuriel** - The goddess of war. The huntress and sentry. She is drawn to conflict and competition alike.

Thus the Senary claimed their domains and for a time, they meddled not with one another. 

##  The Temptress

In those times Synnaren grew tired of watching her brothers and sisters ignore one another. Watching them move past each other without touch. Without care. Without reverence. 

She tired of being ignored.

Synnaren followed Sovalis, vying for his attention. His affection. She tempted him with promises of knowledge.

To Sovalis – “Dear brother, I understand you like no other. The others know of the world and of movement, but we know what it is to feel! Will you not lay with me, so that you may know desire?”

Sovalis knew his sister and her plans. He knew nothing good would come of it and pushed her away.

Synnaren was distraught by her brother’s coldness.  

If she could not have what she desired, she would direct desire toward others. She turned to her siblings, and began to whisper to them in their dreams.

To Aloni-lei – “I see you sister. I see the depth in you that no one knows. Look up, sister. He sees you too. Can you not feel his warmth? Do you not desire it?”

To Anpur-dim – “Dear brother, your toil is endless. You push away darkness and walk past us, watching but never stopping. Look at the waters below. Do they not reflect your splendor? Do you not want to know how they feel?”

She whispered to them day after day. Tempting. Calling to them. Until finally the two could resist no longer. Anpur-dim sank into Aloni-lei, and in their union **Inari-vox** was birthed – the goddess of the inland waters. She is the rain, the rivers, the lakes and streams.

Synnaren watched them again and again, where Anpur-dim sank his warmth into Aloni-lei. She watched their union with excitement turned sour – there was no passion between them. Each day was the same, and this saddened Synnaren.

## The Cyclone

Synnaren once again grew weary. Anpur-dim moved with relentless certainty, never straying from his path. He met Aloni-lei in the same way nearly every night, and Synnaren found it tiresome. So she schemed.

Aloni-lei sat beneath Somar-dani, who brushed over her but never against. Again, Synnaren began whispering to her siblings.

To Somar-dani – “Oh homeless wanderer, I see how you brush across the land. How your fingers curl through the mountains and the trees. Do you not see her beneath you as you pass the empty stretches between? Do you not wonder how your touch might shape her waves?”

To Aloni-lei – “You have such depth, sister, that you should not suffer to wait. All day you sit and do not feel, while he glides over you. Should you not reach?”

Just like before, she whispered day after day. Until finally Aloni-lei reached for him, and Somar-dani took her up. Their passion swirled into a furious tempest, sending her waves crashing against the shores. In this way was born **Kalinexis** – the god of storms. He is a destroyer, cursed to be abandoned by his father even as he abandons his own mother.

Synnaren watched as passion raged then faded, not knowing when it may come again. This saddened Synnaren as she sat with the ache of longing.

## The Age of Men

Synnaren turned her attention to her other siblings.

Annuriel had been tinkering with the clay of the earth, which had been softened with the waters of Inari-vox. She had shaped them into the form of her siblings, whose interests she had failed to capture. One day, Somar-dani passed over the clay, and the clay took its first breath. She would make many more, and named them *man*.

Sovalis was outraged. He reasoned that Annuriel had no right to create beings in the image of the gods, and that it could only lead to disaster. Annuriel dismissed him and continued to craft man in different sizes and colors. She built whole tribes and turned them on one another, delighting in watching them tear each other to pieces. Then she remolded them whole again, only to watch them fight each other once more.

Synnaren spoke to her brother.

To Sovalis – “Sweet brother, leave Annuriel to her playthings. Can you not see that I am here? Will you not be with me?”

But Sovalis would not be lured by her temptations. Instead, Sovalis focused his essence and blessed it unto man so that it would alleviate them from Annuriel’s torment. In some men the knowledge of the world’s pain led to compassion. In others, it spoiled into rot and rage. Man could not hold the wisdom of a god without collapsing under its weight.

Synnaren became jealous of Annuriel. That she could capture her brother’s attention with such trivial things, while he ignored her advances. She began whispering again, this time to distract Annuriel.

To Annuriel – “He does not see you. He will never see you. But there is one who will. One whose passion matches yours. Do not ignore him when he comes.”

Annuriel then came to know desire.

To Kalinexis – “Oh younger one, whose mother released him and whose father stayed not. I see your pain. I see your anger. You are not alone!”

Synnaren watched as fury met fury, war and storm. They danced over men with little regard and when the matter settled, what remained was silence. And so was birthed **Sensa-nai** – goddess of death. 

Synnaren stood next to Sovalis and watched. Sensa-nai carefully knelt next to the men who were dead and dying. She took them in her arms and embraced them, until they gasped their last breath, releasing their spirits. Sensa-nai carefully returned their bodies to the earth.

## The Bargain

Once again Synnaren approached Sovalis. 

To Sovalis – “Do you not see dear brother? We were meant to be together. We are bound to one another, unlike the others entirely. Why do you reject me?”

This time Sovalis spoke to her. He berated her about cursing man with death while offering man nothing. He turned his back to her, and walked away.

Synnaren sought to make amends. She convinced Kalinexis to consort with Inari-vox, flooding the fields so that crops could grow. Thus was born **Nunneran** – god of the harvest. He brings forth the bounty that sustains men and feeds kingdoms.

Still, she could not gain affection from Sovalis, who saw through her schemes.

## The Gods of Man

The wyrd watched the Senary and did not interfere. It saw how they had squabbled, how they had crashed into one another and made things new. The wyrd watched the rise of man and it saw man as a creator, like the gods themselves. Thus was shaped **Grimali Sho-var** - the god of invention. He is the dreamer god. The one who gifts man with inspiration and order, the tools that let man shape the world around him.

Man in turn would create and worship new gods, which was an act of great offense to the Senary and their children. They grew jealous, and began to interfere with the lives of men, to show their power and demand recognition. 

Synnaren saw this in her fellow gods and had an idea. A way to share the burden she’d carried alone. She imparted herself onto them, creating within them a desire to be worshipped. To attract a following as proof of their divine status. Since then they each seek followers and give their blessings or punishments as they see fit.

Synnaren also turned her attention to man, luring him with desire. In some, desire for wealth or power. In others, desire for another human. She touches each one in some way, whether they recognize it or not.

It is her hope that one day, if she is able to show her brother the value of desire, she may finally have someone who desires her.
